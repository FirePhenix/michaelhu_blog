I'm Michael Hu, a software developer who works mostly with Go and Ruby on Rails.

A lot of my day-to-day work sits in the space between Rails applications, Go services, APIs, data structures, and the small design choices that make code easier or harder to maintain over time. I like practical engineering: understanding why something works, where it can break, and how to make the next version a little cleaner.

This blog is where I write down what I'm learning, debugging, and thinking through. Some posts are notes from real problems, some are explanations I wish I had when I first met a topic, and some are just attempts to make backend concepts feel less painful.

I'll also write about ordinary life from time to time. Not everything here has to be technical. Sometimes I may post in French as well, because I'm still learning and practicing the language. Writing is one of the ways I make that learning more real.

I care about simple code, maintainable systems, clear explanations, and the small habit of paying attention. If a post here helps someone avoid a deadlock, simplify a slice or map, learn a new phrase in French, or feel a little less lost, then it has done its job.

You can also find me on GitHub and LinkedIn.
